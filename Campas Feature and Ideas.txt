CAMPAS — CAMPUS NAVIGATION FEATURE BRIEF
==========================================

Tagline: Right building. Right door. Right room.

One-line pitch
--------------
Doorway is a campus navigation tool that solves the confusing final part of getting somewhere on campus: guiding students to the correct entrance, floor, corridor, and room — not merely the right building.

The problem
-----------
Normal maps can get a student close to a building, but they often fail when it matters most. Students may be sent to the wrong side of a building, arrive at a locked entrance, not know which floor a room is on, or be left stranded when a path is closed. This is particularly stressful when someone is late, new to campus, carrying equipment, or needs a step-free route.

Core promise
------------
Search for a room or destination in natural language and receive a simple, trustworthy "last 100 metres" route that tells you exactly where to enter and what to do next.

Ideal user journey
------------------
1. A student searches: "HG12", "The Spark", "closest printer", or "my 10am lecture".
2. Doorway shows the best route to the correct building entrance.
3. At arrival, it switches to a clear arrival card with indoor guidance.
4. The student follows a small number of human instructions and reaches the room.
5. If anything is wrong, they report it in one tap so future directions improve.

MVP FOR A HACKATHON
--------------------
Build a small, polished version for one DCU campus zone and 5–8 popular destinations:
- The Spark
- DCU Library
- Student Union
- One lecture building and a few real rooms
- A café
- A printer
- A quiet study space
- A water refill point, accessible toilet, or lift

Keep the scope tight: one input, one excellent output. A working, visually polished route for a handful of real places is stronger than an unfinished map for the entire campus.

Core features
-------------

1. Room and destination search
   - Search room codes, building names, landmarks, and facilities.
   - Example searches: "HG12", "library", "nearest microwave", "quiet place near me".
   - Accept aliases and the language students actually use, not only formal campus labels.

2. Correct entrance routing
   - Route to a specific entrance rather than the centre of a building.
   - Label entrances clearly: main entrance, reception entrance, accessible entrance, café entrance, east door, etc.
   - Include a photo or visual cue for unfamiliar entrances.

3. The Last 100 Metres arrival card
   When a student reaches the building, show a simple card such as:
   - Use: Main entrance beside the bike racks
   - Go to: Floor 1
   - Turn: Left at reception
   - Landmark: Red staircase / vending machines
   - Destination: HG12, about 45 seconds from the entrance

4. Indoor navigation guidance
   - Provide short, landmark-led instructions instead of pretending indoor GPS is perfectly reliable.
   - Guide through floors, lifts, staircases, corridors, and reception areas.
   - Optional room/floor mini-map for supported buildings.

5. Accessible-route mode
   - Offer a dedicated step-free route, not just the fastest route.
   - Include verified ramps, lifts, automatic doors, accessible entrances, and known obstructions.
   - Add a confidence label for accessibility information; never imply unverified accessibility is certain.

6. Route preferences
   Let users choose the route that suits the situation:
   - Fastest
   - Step-free
   - Well-lit
   - Covered / weather-friendly
   - Avoid stairs

7. Live route status and temporary disruptions
   - Clearly mark paths, gates, entrances, lifts, or corridors as Open, Closed, or Temporarily diverted.
   - Show a "Today on campus" layer for construction, open days, events, queue barriers, and high-footfall areas.
   - Reroute around reported closures.

8. Opening-hours context
   - Show whether a café, office, printer, library area, or building is currently open.
   - Display source and last-verified date rather than guessing.
   - Use clear labels such as Open now, Closed, or Hours may be outdated.

9. One-tap map issue reports
   Let users improve the map in under ten seconds:
   - Entrance wrong
   - Path or gate closed
   - Location moved
   - Not accessible
   - Hours incorrect
   - Other

   Reports go into a review queue. Show report freshness and confirmation status when possible.

10. Trust and freshness labels
   Instead of presenting imperfect information with false certainty, label it:
   - Verified today
   - Official campus information
   - Community-reported 2 hours ago
   - Needs verification
   - May be outdated — check before travelling

11. Useful nearby context
   At a destination, show only helpful nearby essentials:
   - Toilets and accessible toilets
   - Water refill stations
   - Printers
   - Microwaves
   - Lifts
   - Quiet study spaces
   - Bike parking
   - Food and coffee

12. Calm, late-friendly interface
   The urgent route view should show only:
   - Where to go now
   - Time remaining / walking time
   - Which entrance to use
   - One next instruction
   - A clear reroute or report button

Quality-of-life improvements
-----------------------------

A. Natural-language and student-first search
   - "Where is my 10am lecture?"
   - "How do I get to HG12?"
   - "Closest microwave"
   - "Accessible entrance to The Spark"
   - "Quiet place near the library"

B. Saved places and repeat routes
   - Save frequently used rooms, lecture halls, lockers, parking locations, and campus facilities.
   - Show a quick "Get me there" button for favourites.

C. Calendar-aware suggestions
   - Optional integration: identify a lecture location from a calendar event.
   - Suggest when to leave based on walking time.
   - Example: "Your class in HG12 starts at 10:00. Leave in 8 minutes."

D. Photo-based recognition
   - Include entrance photos so students can visually confirm they are in the right place.
   - Allow an uploaded photo to help identify an unclear building sign or entrance (future feature).

E. Community validation
   - Ask simple confirmation questions: "Is this route still correct?"
   - Answers: Yes / Gate closed / Entrance moved / Not accessible / Other.
   - Optionally recognise repeat contributors with a light "Campus Guide" badge.

F. Poor GPS resilience
   - Use GPS outdoors, but switch to landmark-based guidance near and inside buildings.
   - Do not rely on a jumping blue dot for indoor navigation.

G. Clear language and inclusive instructions
   - Avoid campus jargon unless paired with a plain-language explanation.
   - Use simple words, concise directions, high contrast, and readable type.
   - Consider multilingual support and screen-reader-friendly route steps as future extensions.

Common campus-map mistakes
--------------------------

1. Pin placed at the building centre
   Problem: The user arrives on the wrong side of a large building.
   Fix: Use entrance-level destination pins and entrance photos.

2. Paths that are blocked, locked, or under construction
   Problem: The technically shortest route is unusable.
   Fix: Live route-status labels, community reports, and temporary rerouting.

3. Navigation ends at the building door
   Problem: The room is still hard to find inside.
   Fix: The Last 100 Metres arrival card with floor, turn, lift/stairs, landmark, and estimated indoor walking time.

4. Inconsistent building names or confusing room codes
   Problem: Students do not know whether a nickname matches a formal building name.
   Fix: Search aliases, room codes, landmarks, and natural-language queries.

5. Accessibility routes are incomplete or unreliable
   Problem: A suggested path may contain stairs, steep slopes, narrow doors, or a broken lift.
   Fix: Verified step-free routes, accessible-entrance details, lift information, and confidence labels.

6. Outdated or missing opening hours
   Problem: A student walks to a closed facility.
   Fix: Opening status with source and last-updated information.

7. Weak GPS near or inside buildings
   Problem: The user loses trust when their location marker jumps around.
   Fix: Landmark-based guidance and static indoor route instructions.

8. Fastest route is not safest or most comfortable
   Problem: A path might be dark, exposed to rain, or unpleasant late at night.
   Fix: Fastest, well-lit, covered, and step-free route preferences.

9. Event disruption is not represented
   Problem: Open days, maintenance, queues, and roadworks invalidate routes.
   Fix: A prominent Today on Campus layer and time-limited disruption alerts.

10. Errors cannot be reported
    Problem: Users see a wrong route but the map never improves.
    Fix: One-tap reports with a visible verification process.

Possible future features
------------------------
- QR codes on campus signs that open the correct indoor route.
- Lecture timetable import and automatic leave-now reminders.
- Real-time lift outage integration.
- Evening safety companion mode with well-lit routes.
- Crowdsourced queue times for cafés, printers, and library services.
- AR-style directional overlays for the last few steps.
- Facilities filter: find the nearest available printer, microwave, water station, or quiet room.
- Campus staff dashboard to review reports and push official disruption updates.
